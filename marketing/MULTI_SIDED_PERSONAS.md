# LUKU Multi-Sided Personas

**Last Updated:** February 2026  
**Status:** Active – complements `MARKETING_STRATEGY.md` segment “Target Audience Segmentation”

---

## Purpose

This document defines the **multi-sided personas** LUKU serves across the ecosystem and connects each to:

- Their **core jobs-to-be-done**
- Their **pain points**
- How **DIY Mood Boards** and **AI-Driven Recommendations** solve those pains
- The **key activation moments** and metrics per persona

It is designed to sit alongside:

- `MARKETING_STRATEGY.md` – high-level strategy and psychographic segments  
- `FEATURE_HIGHLIGHTS.md` – marketing-ready feature descriptions  
- `MARKETING_QUICK_START.md` – daily/weekly marketer workflow

Here we focus specifically on the **role-based sides** of the platform:

- Consumers / Style Seekers  
- Creators & Pros (stylists, interior/fashion creatives)  
- Brands & Retailers / Sellers

---

## Persona 1 – Style-Seeking Consumer

**Role:** End user planning their own wardrobe, outfits, and lifestyle looks  
**Examples:** Students, young professionals, fashion-curious shoppers

### Jobs-to-Be-Done

- “Help me **see my style** in one place so I feel more confident.”
- “Help me **plan outfits** for upcoming events and everyday life.”
- “Help me **discover new looks** that still feel like ‘me’.”
- “Help me **shop smarter**, not just buy more.”

### Pain Points

- Wardrobe feels chaotic; hard to remember what they own.
- Wastes time every morning deciding what to wear.
- Inspiration is scattered across screenshots, saved posts, and tabs.
- Shopping leads to impulse buys that don’t fit their existing style.

### How LUKU Helps

- **DIY Mood Boards (Albums):**
  - Turn scattered inspiration and saved outfits into **curated mood boards** (e.g. “Work Essentials”, “Weekend”, “Holiday Party”).
  - Make it easy to **plan looks in advance** and see gaps in the wardrobe.
  - Provide a visual “home base” for their evolving style story.

- **AI-Driven Recommendations:**
  - Suggest **complete outfits from their existing wardrobe**.
  - Adapt to feedback (thumbs up/down) to tune into their real preferences.
  - Reduce daily decision fatigue with ready-made outfit ideas.
  - Surface **shoppable additions** that meaningfully extend their wardrobe.

### Activation Moments & Success Signals

- **“Aha” Moment 1:** Creates first mood board with at least 3–5 outfits.
- **“Aha” Moment 2:** Accepts and saves their first AI-recommended outfit.
- **Ongoing Engagement:**
  - Adds new items to wardrobe over time.
  - Regularly updates mood boards for seasons/events.
  - Clicks through to shop recommendations that complement existing items.

**Key Metrics (Consumer Side):**

- % of new signups who:
  - Add ≥1 wardrobe item in first 24–48 hours.
  - Create at least 1 mood board.
  - Save at least 1 AI-generated outfit.
- Weekly active users who:
  - Interact with AI recommendations.
  - View or edit a mood board.

## Persona 2 – Creators & Style Professionals

**Role:** Stylists, creators, and pros using LUKU as a **creative tool** and **client-facing canvas**  
**Examples:** Personal stylists, content creators, wardrobe consultants, interior/lifestyle designers

### Jobs-to-Be-Done

- “Help me **pitch and present style concepts** to clients or followers.”
- “Help me **organize looks and collections** across clients and projects.”
- “Help me **demonstrate expertise** with polished visuals and smart tools.”
- “Help me **save time** on creative exploration and revisions.”

### Pain Points

- Juggling multiple tools (Pinterest, decks, screenshots, DMs) to present ideas.
- Re-creating the same style boards for different clients.
- Difficulty **showing variations fast** (e.g. small tweaks for different budgets or preferences).
- Hard to keep a **living portfolio** of work that’s easy to update.

### How LUKU Helps

- **DIY Mood Boards:**
  - Act as **client/project boards**: one board per client, project, or campaign.
  - Enable side-by-side comparison of concepts (“Look A vs Look B”).
  - Serve as a **living portfolio** of completed looks and case studies.
  - Easy to share with clients or communities (now or in future sharing features).

- **AI-Driven Recommendations:**
  - Accelerate **concept exploration** (e.g. “capsule wardrobe”, “brand shoot looks”).
  - Generate multiple variations quickly; creator curates and refines.
  - Provide data-backed suggestions that can **justify creative decisions** (“AI also validates this combination”).

### Activation Moments & Success Signals

- **“Aha” Moment 1:** Creates a client/project mood board populated with looks.
- **“Aha” Moment 2:** Uses AI recommendations to **generate a set of options** for a brief and saves at least one.
- **Ongoing Engagement:**
  - Re-uses templates/boards for multiple clients.
  - Shares boards externally (when sharing is enabled) as portfolio or deliverables.

**Key Metrics (Creator/Pro Side):**

- Number of boards tagged as “Client” / “Project” per creator.
- AI recommendations saved into pro-owned boards.
- Frequency of revisiting boards (updates over time).
- Conversion from “regular user” to “pro workflows” (board complexity, naming patterns, etc.).

## Persona 3 – Brands & Retailers / Sellers

**Role:** Businesses listing products and selling through the LUKU marketplace (via seller dashboard & business portal)  
**Examples:** Boutique brands, online fashion shops, curated resale sellers

### Jobs-to-Be-Done

- “Help me **reach buyers who are ready to purchase**, not just scroll.”
- “Help me **show how my products fit into real outfits** and style stories.”
- “Help me **measure performance** and optimize inventory & promotion.”
- “Help me **reduce the manual grind** of DMs, payments, and operations.”

### Pain Points

- Social platforms deliver likes and views, but **few real purchases**.
- Algorithm changes make **reach unpredictable**.
- Ops are manual: DMs, screenshots, manual payments, spreadsheets.
- Hard to connect products to **styled looks** that drive confidence and conversion.

### How LUKU Helps

- **DIY Mood Boards:**
  - Let sellers create **“Shop the Board” collections**: e.g. “New Arrivals”, “Occasionwear”, “Holiday Drop”.
  - Show products **in the context of complete outfits**, not just as isolated items.
  - Support co-marketing content: “As seen in this board → shop each piece”.

- **AI-Driven Recommendations:**
  - Power “Complete the Look” experiences: when a buyer saves or views an outfit, AI can surface **complementary products** from sellers.
  - Generate **AI-curated collections** (e.g. “Under $50 picks”, “Neutral capsule from Brand X”).
  - Help sellers understand **what combinations resonate** via engagement and conversion.

### Activation Moments & Success Signals

- **“Aha” Moment 1:** Completes onboarding and lists first 5–10 products.
- **“Aha” Moment 2:** Sees their products appear in **AI-driven outfit or “complete the look”** flows and mood boards.
- **Ongoing Engagement:**
  - Uses seller dashboard analytics to adjust pricing, stock, or promos.
  - Creates or contributes to boards that feature their products.
  - Participates in **campaigns** (e.g. “New Sellers”, seasonal collections).

**Key Metrics (Seller/Brand Side):**

- Number of active business accounts and **products listed**.
- % of SKUs that appear in:
  - Mood boards (seller-created or user-created)
  - AI-driven outfits or “complete the look” suggestions.
- Conversion from:
  - Product view → add to wishlist/cart → order.
- Repeat order rate per seller.

## Cross-Persona Alignment Around Mood Boards & AI

To keep the ecosystem coherent, we anchor messaging around the **same two hero capabilities**, interpreted per persona:

- **DIY Mood Boards**
  - Consumer: “My style story & outfit planner.”
  - Creator/Pro: “My client boards and visual portfolio.”
  - Brand/Seller: “My shoppable lookbooks and ‘shop the board’ experiences.”

- **AI-Driven Recommendations**
  - Consumer: “My always-on personal stylist.”
  - Creator/Pro: “My fast idea generator and style assistant.”
  - Brand/Seller: “My engine for ‘complete the look’ and AI-curated collections.”

This alignment ensures:

- Campaigns can be tailored by persona **without fragmenting the core story**.
- Product and UX decisions can be evaluated against **shared activation metrics** (board creation, AI usage, and downstream commerce behavior).

## How to Use This Document

- **Marketing:**  
  - Use these personas when writing copy, selecting creatives, and deciding which benefits to emphasize in each channel.
  - Map each campaign brief to **which persona(s)** it primarily serves.

- **Product & UX:**  
  - Validate new features against at least one persona’s jobs-to-be-done.
  - Ensure mood board and AI flows support both **end-user creativity** and **commerce outcomes** for sellers/brands.

- **Analytics & Growth:**  
  - Segment activation and retention metrics by persona where possible (consumer vs pro vs seller).
  - Tie experiments (onboarding, feature tours, campaigns) back to persona-specific activation moments listed above.
